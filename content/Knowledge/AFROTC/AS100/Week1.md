## New Cadet Orientation

Originally, I thought I could stop by anytime from 10am-2pm– but I fortunately realized my mistake with 30 minutes left on the clock. After I showed up a few minutes late, I signed the required documents for the WINGS account. Ironically, I already had mine signed and printed– so I need to shred those later. After all, I'm responsible for myself. Part of that responsibility is making sure that I don't leave sensitive info lying around.   

For the most part, everything's already dealt with. While I didn't have my appointment this week, it'll definitely get resolved. 


## Commander's Call

This time, I decided to show up 15 minutes early since I didn't know the biking path for Bromley Hall. In return, I walk into my Seniors' line of sight and get reunited with my cohort. The neat part here was that everyone else gathered around the **other** door. For future reference, make sure that the rest of your fellow cadets aren't lingering around outside **before** you walk inside. 

Anyways with that aside, the Commander's call was packed in terms of info. I'm going to have to revisit that slideshow eventually, but those 38 minutes weren't in vain. Looking back at those photos, I should've looked at the way everyone else posed so I could fit in better. Looking at those photos is like looking at a hairline fracture. Even if it's barely visible, i's still a flaw. 

After the photos, I spent a few minutes fumbling around with my GPS until I figured out the way back home. But before that, I stopped by Nelson and ate with some of the other cadets that gathered around. 

## AST1010 

In the morning when I looked at teams, I was wondering why TSgt Goode mentioned civilian clothes. It only hit me two hours later that I forgot about AST1010 entirely. Thankfully, I was able to catch up quickly after realizing that. Still, I put it in my calendar since it was the only class that I was missing. 

For the Heritage series, I chose Chief Master Sargent **Etchberger**. I bow my head in respect to all of those men interred, but Etchberger's death is one that I aspire towards.
