AG5820:Student Government

Date of Submission:10/13/25

Definition: Administrative Guideline 5820(AG5820) is a policy enacted by GJPS’s Board of Education regarding the formation of student governments and the guidelines that they must adhere to with Policy Manual 5820(PO5820) acknowledging the purpose of a student government and recognizes Student Councils as a Student Government. The mention of PO5820 is to avoid any conflicts regarding the usage of technicalities.

Regarding the Student Council and AG5820:

The Student Council of GJPS qualifies as a Student Government under AG5820. As such, it must comply with all guidelines made by the Board of Education. However, there are concerns regarding its non-compliance with board policies along with structural issues and transparency concerns as outlined below.

Guidelines 1&2:

AG5820’s first and second guidelines relate to its establishment and functions, highlighting the importance of a constitution.

“A. The organization, operation, and scope of the student government body shall be defined in a written constitution developed through effective student participation with selected school advisors, and approved by the principal.  
B. Student government organizations shall function with respect to the provisions of their constitution and bylaws.”

Issue: The constitution is not accessible to the student body itself. How can the student body hold their representatives accountable if they don’t understand the government designed to represent them? This lack of knowledge regarding the Student Council itself inhibits the student body’s ability to self-police their government. It removes a check on the power of the Student Council.

The governmental system of this Student Council is a democratic government– a government for the people, by the people. An essential quality of a democratic government is its ability to be policed by the people that it governs over. If a government can no longer be influenced and kept in check by its own people, is it truly a democratic government?

Non-compliance with Guideline 5: Failure to hold Open Meetings.

I find that the current Student Council and their predecessors have failed their obligation under AG5820’s fifth guideline which states the following:

“The student government organization shall be responsive to the needs and interests of all students, and shall conduct open meetings to assure maximum involvement of all students.”

I have been enrolled in GLHS for three months but I have yet to see an open meeting held by the Student Council. Additionally, I have searched online to check whether the previous Student Councils have held any open meetings and have found no evidence of them doing so– thus empowering my claim.

This lack of open meetings takes away the student body’s ability to provide input to the operations of the Student Council itself, thus further creating more distance between the Student Council and their constituents. Additionally, it further widens the power gap between the elected representative and the student body– the students' lack of voice only serves to empower their voices.

The Student Council’s failure to implement this guideline in turn affects another guideline.

Structural Non-compliance and Need for Reform:

I find that the Student Council has essentially deprived the rights of the student body to act as oversight of the council itself and has taken their voice away– giving rise to an unequal relationship between the Student Council and the student body.

The student body’s voice has been diminished. Their only means to influence the Student Council is their ability to vote– an indirect influence. A vote alone cannot represent the true voice of the student body. A student may elect a representative that they are in agreement with, yet there are nuances that separate the two– after all, the voter and elected representatives are ultimately two different people.

In this current system, the student body is only responsible for electing their representatives and only interacts with the Student Council in a passive manner–giving the representatives an oversized voice while the student body remains mute. Can the Student Council truly claim to be a Student Government if their very structure inhibits the participation of the student body?

The Student Council in its current form is insulated from the will of students– the only check responsible for keeping them in balance being the principal. There is no need for these representatives to consider whether their decisions would be ones that the student body would support or be against, for the current system protects them well from the repercussions of their actions. If the student body cannot hold the council accountable for any of their actions or words, then the Student Council alienates itself from its constituents, ultimately denying its purpose to serve the student body.

I concur that the Student Council acts for the good of the student body, however the good that they claim to act for is a good that only the Council itself knows. With such a lack of transparency, the Council has a dangerous potential to paint the narrative as one that would favour the Council and its choices. While there is no evidence of this potential being realized, this potential must be smoldered. Without transparency, the Student Council cannot truly embody the principles of a democratic government– for the majority of the operations that the Student Council operates remains unknown to the student body.

While the Student Council may argue that students have the capability to contact a Council member in order to relay their concerns, this is a passive means of input and still acts in non-compliance with the 5th Guideline as it does not constitute an open meeting. The student body lacks knowledge regarding the timings and intervals of the meeting of the Student Council. This lack of knowledge is indirectly acting in non-compliance with the 5th Guideline as it inhibits the participation of the student body in Student Council meetings.

The 6th Guideline is still not being complied with as there is no explicit mechanism or formal system kept in place to satisfy this guideline. It can be further argued that the Student Council is partially following the 6th Guideline and has fulfilled its duty. However, within that line of reasoning, it transforms the Student Council’s non-compliance into a purposeful one, thus elevating this matter in its severity as that line of reasoning signifies that the Student Council was already aware of the non-compliance yet allowed it to remain in place, thus showing deliberate intent.

As it currently stands, I have made the assumption that the Student Council is likely to not have knowledge of these policies due to the exceptional circumstances that led me to discover AG5820 and submit this memorandum.

Additionally, it is the Student Council’s obligation to notify the student body of the rights that they are entitled to. If the student body is not informed of their rights, the Student Council still stands in non-compliance with AG5820 as they have allowed this ignorance to persist, showing a lack of transparency.

Even if the Student Council had already established formal channels or mechanisms to ensure the compliance of Guideline 6, the fact that the student body has no understanding of the rights they are entitled to under AG5820 shows that this policy is only being implemented symbolically and that it is not actually being implemented in practice.

While the Student Council in recent times has made efforts to encourage student involvement, I question the purpose of this involvement. I will not come to a verdict regarding this as I lack sufficient evidence. But a question must be posed, is this involvement one simply for publicity or is there a well documented reason behind the Student Council’s efforts that benefits the student body? This excludes charitable work as I acknowledge the Student Council’s contributions and have no disagreements regarding it.

Once again, I am thwarted by my inability to access the Student Council’s archived content so I must instead pay closer attention and start to document the Council’s actions myself in order for a proper conclusion to be made.

However, the issues that I mentioned regarding the Student Council’s non-compliance with AG5820 and raised concerns are structural in nature– the blame lies with the structure of the Student Council. While these elected representatives may have failed their obligation regarding their knowledge of this policy, they are not to be held responsible for the structure that they have inherited from preceding Student Councils.

However, the current Student Council should be held responsible to amend themselves to address these perceived violations.

Proposals:

In order to address the concerns that I have raised, I would like to propose some measures that the council itself may take into consideration and ultimately pass these measures after expanding on it

The dissemination of the Constitution of the Student Council:

My proposal is that the Student Council creates an online pdf that contains the constitution in a legible form and is responsible for the medium of its dissemination by using both digital and physical resources available to them.

The establishment of Open Meetings:

My proposal here is that the Student Council conducts open meetings and to invite students to take part in these open meetings. The interval of these meetings should be one that does not overly burden the Council yet allows the student body to provide their inputs in a frequent manner.

These meetings should be a hybrid of an online and offline meeting. The Student Council can meet physically in a room optimized for the digital aspects of the meeting. This relieves the Student Council some logistical concerns if the Open Meetings were mandated as physical meetings. Additionally, this could be interpreted as a mandatory requirement due to the wording of Guideline 5 including “to assure maximum involvement of all students”.

Regarding members of the student body joining the physical meeting, I leave that to the discretion and wisdom of the Student Council.

If a member of the student body wishes to speak, they must schedule this with the Communications Officer before the meeting or at the beginning of the meeting.

That member will be given the opportunity to speak at the beginning of the meeting or at the end of the meeting. They will be allotted 3 minutes for their speaking time, a time that can be revoked or penalized by the Presiding Officer. Additionally, this speaker will have to follow the rules of decorum.

I would advise the Student Council to take into account AG5723. It is an administrative guideline regarding the expression of students. The Student Council should draft up legislation in accord with that policy to moderate the speaker’s expression.

This proposal rectifies the non-compliance of Guideline 5 and partially rectifies the non-compliance of Guideline 6.

Phase 2:

I would like to go a step further with this proposal by proposing that the Student Council’s regular meetings should be open meetings, allowing for the student body to gain a better understanding of the operations of the Council itself.

However, there would be exceptions to this. Any special meeting conducted by the Student Council may remain as a closed meeting. Additionally, any meeting that involves confidential information or requires discretion may also remain as a closed meeting. However, this said reason must be recorded and published.

Public Records:

My proposal is that the Student Council henceforth adopt a public record policy regarding their official documents, agenda, meeting minutes,resolutions,meeting schedules, the respective members of the Student Council, and all other relevant information.

The Student Council is to implement this by publishing these records online. Additionally, a public records request form shall be made for students who are interested in documents that were not published. This policy would address the concerns I had regarding transparency and accountability.

Additionally, another internal record is to be created. The purpose of this record is to contain all comprehensive information regarding the Student Council that was not released in the public records. The access of this record is restricted to the Student Council and is to be passed onto successive Student Councils.

The formation of a mechanism that fully rectifies the non-compliance of Guideline 6.

While my second proposal partially satisfies Guideline 6, I would like to propose an online form that students can use to recommend guidelines for their own behavior.

The identity of the petitioner that submitted these guidelines is to be stored temporarily in order to account for any malicious guidelines. Furthermore, the petitioner’s identity is to be classified as confidential information accessible to the President, Vice-President, and the assigned position for this role.

After the omission of their identities, these recommendations are to be addressed in the next meeting and would be subject to a vote on whether to further consider it. The reason for the omission is to ensure that the Council members remain impartial and are not affected by any personal ties to the petitioner.

This task is to be delegated to a suitable position along with an oath to keep the identities of the petitioners a secret. The penalty for breaking this oath would be an impeachment hearing in which a panel of council members along with faculty decide on the outcome of this hearing. This is to ensure that the accountability of the Student Council cannot be called into question.

Of course, there are exceptions to be made case-by-case and a list of allowed exceptions is to be made. The specifics of such a list is a task that I leave for the Student Council to fulfill if this proposal is considered further.

The expansion and clarification of the Student Council:

This proposal is a general proposal due to limited transparency regarding the structure and operations of the Student Council.

What I have understood, the Student Council contains all four grade levels. However, with the exception of the President and Vice-President, there are six officers of the Council– each of them given a separate domain: Communications, Transition Coordinator, Diversity, Social Leadership, Wellness, and Philanthropy.

I will assume that these domains are committees and that these officers are the head of their respective committee. At the time of my writing, there is no information regarding the Treasurer and the rest of the positions of any other committees or sub-committees in the student council, so there is no way for me to know whether the proposals I have in mind have already been implemented in spirit. This is an additional reason for which I campaign transparency for.

With the current structure of the Student Council, the Officer most suitable for the implementation and execution of these proposals would be the Communications Officer. However, this dilutes the responsibilities given to the Officer and would overburden them.

I propose that the position of Records Officer is created along with their associated Records committee. This Officer would be delegated with the implementation and execution of the proposals that I have already proposed within this memorandum.

Unlike other roles within the Council, the Records Officer is an appointed role with the possibility of their committee being formed with appointed members rather than elected members. The term of the Records Officer is of 1 school year with the Records Officer having the obligation to write their insights gained from their position in order to aid their successor.

A succession protocol must be enacted for the Records Officer due to their position being a more specialized one in its nature, thus having a higher learning curve. There is no term limit regarding the members of the Records committee as the Records Officer is responsible for their appointment and firing.

However, the Records Officer must present a due reason for the firing and must answer to the Council. In the event that the Records Officer fails this obligation, they shall be subject to a disciplinary hearing with an internal memo regarding this misbehavior.

The applicant for the position of Records Officer would be responsible for their own nomination after they pass a typing test and a trial in which they partake in a council meeting to showcase their ability to document. If they have fulfilled both requirements, the Student Council is obligated to vote on their appointment.

This is due to the need of the members of the records committees to have a sufficient level of skill and proficiency. This requirement would ensure that the committee would be well-equipped to document the official actions taken by the council as opposed to electing an individual who lacks the skills for this role.

While this member may be a part of the Council in name, their role is akin to a bureaucratic role than a representative role as the student body plays an indirect role in their appointment. While they may don the moniker of Council member, they do not share the same level of authority and power that an elected Student Council member holds due to the origin of their position.

The Records Committee's nature is impartial, to not be affected by a personal bias or a subjective bias regarding the policies of the Student Council themselves. Its members have no vote regarding the policy decisions made by the Student Council. They are expected to remain secretive regarding their activities due to the sensitive nature of their work. The Records Committee solely reports to the Records Officer. The Records Officer reports to the President and Advisor. In the event that the President is unavailable or has delegated their authority, the Records Officer reports to the Vice-President. The Records Officer’s power and authority is equivalent to the other Officers with the exception of his voting power. Like their committee members, the Records Officer does not have a vote.

There are two possible directions that could be developed from here regarding the appointment of the members of the Records Committee.

The first option is for the Records Officer to be responsible for the appointment of the members of the Records Committee. The second option is that the members of the Records Committee are elected.

My suggestion is a combination of the two, in which the Records Officer is to be responsible for their appointment and the Student Council is responsible for their confirmation vote after the Records Officer ensures their competency.

I also propose that the creation of a Judicial Officer to act as the internal watchdog of the Student Council regarding policies. The Judicial Officer is responsible for the oversight of the policies that the Student Council enacts, ensuring that the policy does not conflict with a Board policy. The Judicial Officer has the burden of proof regarding the policy’s conflict with board policy. If their findings have determined a conflict, they are delegated with the authority to either veto the entire policy or line-veto it, essentially vetoing the part of the policy that stands in non-compliance with the policies of the Board. The Judicial Officer is subject to oversight from the Advisor and Principal.

Once again, this position is an appointed one in which the qualifications for this position must be the ability to comprehend board stature and to possess the integrity required of this Officer position. Originally, I intended for the Records Officer to hold this authority, however I believed that it would be excessive.

I leave it up to the Student Council on whether they wish to go with my initial tendency to delegate this authority to the Records Officer or to create a stand-alone Officer role.

Additionally, I propose that the current respective committees’ roles and responsibilities are to be formally published in a litigable manner as I do not believe that their names alone can be considered acceptable to explain their functions.

Additionally, I would like further elaboration of the roles and responsibilities of the other members of the Student Council in order to better draft proposals in relation to the personnel structure of the Student Council.

A potential concern I have about this proposal is that my assumption that the titles that six Officers possess are stand-alone in nature and that they do not head committees formed by non-officer Student Council members. Once again, this confusion is due to a lack of transparency regarding the operations of the Student Council and their structure.

A website dedicated to the operations of the Student Council:

I propose that the Student Council is given a website in order to facilitate proposals 1,3,4.

Gives the Student Council more leeway for the content of the said website, as Social Media is not an effective way of spreading detailed information.

Is more accessible to students in general considering that updates regarding the student council can only be received via Social Media and Lincoln Live from what I have observed. This would result in increased engagement from the student body.

Makes the image of the Student Council appear to be more professional.

For the implementation of this website, this proposal is to be refined further. Cooperation with the district’s technology department is highly recommended for this proposal. The website should be a well-designed one that allows for the Student Council to add text onto the website dynamically and to be able to later modify it.
