Nine months ago, I wrote [[32-Identity|'Identity']]. An essay that served as an indictment to both readers and author. For a man who venerates our courts, it is a shameful one. I wrote myself as a prosecutor, as a judge, as a victim– all of these roles meshed into one. 

So, in [[33-Thoughts_2|Thoughts-2]], I struck 961 words from the official record. But they still remain. I will not revise history to change my image, as I see it as a slope. If I'm so willing to revise history, what stops me from wanting to rewrite myself– to amputate parts of myself? If anything, my image should be my shadow– I shouldn't be bound to my image. 

But like I said before, I am not writing a static collection. This body of essays is a living constitution, so it'll always grow. The goal of [[46-Condition]] is to expand the concept of loneliness that I previously wrote back in January. 

Oftentimes, we treat loneliness like a short-term condition– one that'll go away once we spend time with people. To a certain extent, it's true. But, I find that it's more akin to a chronic condition– one that'll persist throughout a lifetime. 










After all, people will come and people will go. That much is certain in our lives. Even if they do remain in our lives, we merely exist in each other's lives. We may share it, but we will never fully understand another person's perspective as if it was our own.
