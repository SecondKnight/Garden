Another contradiction has manifested itself. I am alone, but not. I may be a part of them, but I can't say the same for myself. here are only so few things that I truly have a part in. 

I am Black. My blood and skin color being proof of this. Yet I find myself estranged from my own skin. If I knew no better, then I would ask if I was the wrong type of black. But, I know my people exist somewhere– places where I can't see. It's a shame, but I won't change the shade of my skin just to fit in. 

I am also Indian, my likeness being proof of this. Its a bit ironic, that I look like either race but not both. When I came back from India, I got hit with a subtle question. Some of us claim to be Indian just from our blood. They didn't breathe in our native pollution, but their compass points them back to us. In my eyes, they are like biryani without the masala– blood established legitimacy, not heritage. If you don't practice this heritage in some way, or haven't experienced India– are you truly Indian? Or just an imposter wearing our skin?

I have friends, but I seem to exist as their attachments– not as a fully fledged person. When i speak, I must raise my voice and speak again and again– lest my words go unheard. A fact that I loathe and resent, a constant in all that I do. Once a conversation has more than me and them, I turn translucent. 

Tell me, what difference is there between the sound of battering ram and the sight a door being rammed? Why am I forced to break the door, to act like an intruder when my presence is perfectly warranted.  Other times, I keep my distance or busy myself. After all, I know where people's interests lie– their eyes aren't on me. Even when they look at me, it's not because they hold a special interest in me– it's simply convenient for them.

Tell me, if I didn't start our interactions– how could they end theirs? While I cannot claim to know their minds, it very well could've been another man sitting in that chair. Casualty spoke, and I answered. I am a individual, just not in moments like these. Most of my qualities are particularly unsuited in what little company I have. It's annoying. I can't tell if I've always been this generic or this was circumstantial– but nonetheless I remain aware of this. 

One day, I will lose my temper. I care not if I die before that day, but I know it'll happen. After all, I am a man– a man whose patience wanes in time. I love people, but I tire of these antics of theirs. My conscience tells me that this is incidental, that they never meant for this to happen. But does it not make it worse? That your deafness is a natural trait, that I didn't matter enough to suppress it– am I not owed this morsel of attention? 

It's interesting how you can find love in the smallest gestures, courtesies that should've already been normalized. I'd call myself envious, but envy consists of two components. To covet what you see in others, and to seize it for yourself. I may want what others have, but I can't stoop that low. So instead, I am bitter.   

Bitter that some of us are blessed with the presence of others, that they're always surrounded by warmth. While I live on rays, they bathe in uninterrupted beams of light. Yet, I know well that they too have their struggles– but even that doesn't soothe my heart. We both may be alone, but their loneliness isn't the same as mine– it may be more or less, but never the same. 

Just as they don't linger in the shade for too long, I don't stay in the light for too long. It's like drinking caffeine. Once you start to get used to drinking it daily, you can't stop and fall deeper into this cycle of dependence. If I had a constant source of light, then perhaps I could let it happen– but that's a matter for the future. While suffering is another constant of life, this particular one is unnecessary. 

This dagger is already pricking my heart. If I were to accept this pain, then it becomes a sword. Tell me, if my chest cavity is already cramped– just what will this sword pierce? Sigh, perhaps it'd be better if I were more ignorant– if I thought less. Perhaps that's another one of my flaws. To ponder instead of acting. But, it's not a flaw. It is a symptom of what I call a blessing and a curse– wisdom. 

I know my flaws, but I know not how to resolve them. There is so much that I lack, yet my efforts are like scooping sand– only a few grains remain in grasp. I recognize that this perspective of mine is an elevated one, and that others may not have the same clarity as me. You may call me vain, but these words of mine are a reflection– one that shows this face of mine. 

I could continue this particular essay with the amount of embers I have left, references from all that I've written and all that I haven't. But what good is it if I'm the only one who knows? It's like building an home, but getting a house instead– a place that lacks a human's touch. Sure I may be human, but I can't smudge my fingers over every crevice. 

At this point, when I write the word 'You'– I'm writing to another version of myself. He may be my future or an alternative version of myself– but one fact is certain.  Here, it's just me. Just me and my pride. Of everything I have in this life, every word here is an inscription of joy and growth– ones that won't be read. 

I've begun to realize something. Elsewhere, I can say that I'm isolated. In this space of words, I can write that I am alone. Even if I'm isolated, the fact remains that I still interact with others and experience that surface level of affinity. In this world of words, only and God exist.
