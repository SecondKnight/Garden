Preface:

Normally, I wouldn’t write a preface for my pieces. However, due to the sensitivity of this topic, I believe that it’s necessary to do so. This is about individual behaviour and is not linked to any specific group of people. It’s meant to be applicable to those who identify themselves as human. There’s a reason why my writing was vague at some points, especially when I referred to religion. It is a critique of behaviour, not of minorities nor majorities. The morality that I, by extension by work– is a morality that remains equal. It would do you well to remember that majorities and minorities are subjective if we do not limit ourselves to the States.  
  
When I say “You”, know that it is inclusive in its nature– for it serves a warning for those who aren’t truly being referred to, it serves as omen for those who engage in the duplicitous activity that I refer to, and as a reminder and tribute to those who have removed this soot-covered lenses. For me, it serves as all three in a way. For these words are akin to a bond placed upon me– I must uphold myself to a higher standard than those that I criticize.   
  
At times, I may use “You” in a hostile manner, I ask that you take no offense to it as it’s conditional in nature– a condition that I doubt most people would qualify for. 

  
The original nature of my writing has changed to a nature that could be viewed as extreme if taken literally. 

  

This is an exercise of creative writing, not meant to be interpreted literally. When I refer to the actions of a sword, I refer to legally permissible methods. An example of this would be peacefully protesting within constitutional rights, litigation, and political activity. I do not condone violence in any way, shape or form. I will reiterate time and time again, my words are metaphoric in nature and that this writing is focused in its nature. Thus, it does not serve as a true representation of my thoughts.

There is a trend that has been adopted, one that infuriates me just by thinking about it– a trend where the oppressed have now become oppressors. Some of us who knew discrimination intimately against their will have now embraced it with open arms. I find that the behavior of these former victims to be more abhorrent than the men who started this practice.  
  
Even then, there are those amongst us who discriminate against their own kind– believing that this severing of ties would allow new ties to form. Yet the fate of such men is to become drifters– cursed to never belong with others, to be a solitary existence. A fate of a turncoat– to belong to nothing.

  
For all I have understood, I see nothing that exonerates these people, those who wish to leave matching scars upon another. It truly boggles my mind to see such people exist in the first place– a sense of anger and disgust pervading my being. They seem to believe that their history has entitled them to discriminate against others– an interpretation so twisted that its tethering rope splintered long ago.   
  
They stand on top of their arrogance as they cast judgement on those dissimilar to them. If they were to be discriminated against, then they would be the first to cry and claim to be victims– this apparent hypocrisy being one that boils my blood.  
  
I worry that this prejudice is one that will entrench itself further– for this poison to seep deeper into our foundations. The age of the Civil Rights movement is less than a century but it seems that another movement shall rise up and eclipse the 1960s’ changes if we continue on this ruinous path.  
  
We have studied the failures that earned themselves a place in history’s shrine– the heritage of both nation and man yet remain remiss– scorning history and tempting it to repeat itself once more. While I recognize that this possibility is an uncertain one, I refuse to entertain it in the slightest. 

  

Such a mindset should be broken down into components– to be reformed and built anew. For hatred is a concept quite dear to me; A feeling that should not be directed to such baseless concepts– it is equivalent to viewing an entire nation with tinted lenses, to only see that which furthers an agenda. It is a notion that only serves to pollute the purity of such conviction.

  

I cannot fathom any possible justification for their words except for ignorance and hubris. Ignorant of the suffering of their own people, ignorant of the stories of their ancestors, ignorant of the power of the words that lie on their tongue, ignorant of the consequences of their speech, ignorant of people in general, ignorant of their own shortfalls, an ignorance that they feel no shame for– a shame that has absconded with their morals. 

  

Have we not learnt from those who once fought for dignity? Have you no pride– to lower yourself by embracing the vices that great men abolished? Where has the honour that your forefathers have cultivated gone? You have gone and raked shame unto those who liberated us– those men tokened their blood as currency for the breath you take yet you defile this tender with your acts.  
  

Their hubris is one that judges a whole due to the actions of a fraction of a group– to use that fraction as a basis for their erroneous judgement. Their hubris is their complacency in the regard of their knowledge– forgetting imparted wisdom, wisdom of men whose warnings shall be proven true. For darkness begets darkness, the shade of discrimination shall only beget a different shade– a shade that provides a cover for those who sequestered their malice away, waiting for a cause to wield their hate. 

  
But for me to paint such a broad coat would ultimately be a self-defeating act– contradicting my own words, thus making me no better than those who I sought to address and hold accountable. After all, while we may try to divide ourselves into two sides for the sake of simplicity– we are complex creatures, a complexity that extends to our thoughts. One that fractures those two sides, creating facets with shades of grey.  
  
Ignorance in its purest form is not a sin– yet I still view it as an evil as man’s innate nature is to seek knowledge– irrespective of topic or worth, for the worth of knowledge is subjective. To remain ignorant is a sin that only serves to hinder humanity with a naive incompetence– one in which purposeful malice could never hope to match. 

  

For they do not understand the power they wield nor the consequences they incur. The quote 'The road to hell is paved with good intentions.' resonates deeply, as their intent is the only component that could be understood– the very same intent that absolves them of their sin by the slightest of margins.  
  

When their ignorance becomes tangible, we must not allow them to retain their blissful ignorance. If they wish to remain in such squalor,so be it. Yet the moment that they attempt to manifest their ignorance, it should be rebuked– to challenge their lack of knowledge. From these challenges, they shall learn to either jump out of the well they remain entombed– lest we seal them in this chosen tomb.

  

To accept the right of expression is to accept that burden of consequences– chains that shall hold you accountable. The life of a fool must not remain unchallenged for as long as they wish to spread their poison in society. Let their fallout be a contained one– ensuring that others will not follow them and tempt fate into reaping them, be it a shard or the entire mirror.

  

Let them learn of the true cost of their words, to let them recognize the blush on their cheeks as shame, to let their hubris be burnt by the flames of knowledge. For this flame shall only burn once with age as its fuel– to become a flame whose ashes retain life, serving as a purified foundation for them to attain salvation.  
  
There are those that will never feel this flame– for their age shall become a fuel that shall swallow them whole, leaving them bereft of all they had. They will be consumed by their own ambition, for they know not why it burns– it’s smoke only serving to blind them further. 

  

There are those with grace, content with their knowledge yet no longer seeking it. They too are ignorant– yet they understand this notion and accept it, remaining with their kindred brethren until it is time for their departure. If I were to wear a hat, I would tip it to show my respect for such humility.

  

The sufferings of both you and your forefathers does not entitle you to bear your fangs towards others. Pray tell, if your ancestors still roamed on this earth, their disappointment would be enough to silence you. After all, you may not feel the shame that should be felt after committing such hate– but the shame you bring on them is vivid enough for them to ascend to the afterlife once more.  
  
The freedom you have today was not for you to don the mask of oppression, but to wield the voice of equality. Lives were not lost for you to spit on their graves with the blatant racism you show today. By enjoining this evil, you have nullified these sacrifices by embracing a hate that was never truly yours.  
  
The behaviour of a few has tainted our actions as a whole, perverting our virtues by casting a shadow that we are forced to drag– one that shall continue to be dragged long after our passing. It is such behaviour that I revile, along with our flaws that have only served to feed this monstrosity. It is a betrayal of our ideals, of our preachings, of our justice that we have delivered onto ourselves. 

  

We are a people who do not tolerate even a whisper of discrimination from others– a voice that we have snuffed out countless times in the past, a whisper that we have extinguished in our present, and a message that our future shall not permit. Yet such behaviour exists amongst our own kindred folk, setting a dangerous precedent.  
  
Why are we so blind to our mistakes? Why must we accept this trend– a trend that I would rather die than to even consider the notion of accepting? I recognize my stance to be an opinionated one, a stance that I am not hesitant to die for– for if a man has nothing to die for, do they truly live or are they simply imitating?  
  
I have grown weary of this arrogance, of this complacency that exists within us– cause and effect are not to be denied, one that you attempt to distort with failed trickery. You may claim victimhood– yet you shall always always oppress yourself by engaging in this behavior, maintaining the paradox of both victim and aggressor.  
  
I am tired of those whose sparks have long been lost– for it is their lack which causes my spark to burn on an intensity bordering on hysteria? My people, I ask you, where is your spark? Where is your passion? Where is the flame that was passed onto– your flame of heritage? For I know my people to be a valiant people yet their voices no longer bless my ears.   
  
O my nation, are we truly willing to remain in silence as dissent is sowed, to allow the hatred that our soldiers buried with their bodies to resurface?  For silence is no different than complicity, a silence that condemns you in the eyes of the nation– breaching the covenant you had with it at birth.  
  
O my country, your brave sons of liberty fought not once but twice to liberate ourselves from this hate– at home and away at foreign lands. Their bounty was death, not rememberence– some still remain laid at rest in these foreign lands, to remain separated from their nation even after death.  
  
Both nation and people bled, and I am expected to remain silent as I watch these corpses scream yet have no voice. For what else shall strike a deeper pain into these men other than the betrayal of their sacrifice. The blood of my predecessors set a standard for a future that failed to uphold it. 

  

For such hatred is a crime in all but name– for our nation delegated this judgement to the people. A judgement that has remained consistent for decades until now– for the people have forgotten the contours of their corpses.  
  
It is this madness that gives cause for my spark to rage– the madness that has overcome my people. A madness that disguised itself as tolerance– perverting the founding fathers’ intent, for the politicians of today consist of a taint sustained by this tolerance, acting as a shield for this hatred to prosper.  
  
There is a flame in my heart for my own people, for their lax attitudes and hollow shells of stoicism only serve as blinders– one that they willingly wear and have the gall to boast about it. While my heart is set alight, I would be hard-pressed to find a similar spark in such a group of people. I mourn for the death of such sparks, for they are smoldered by their own hand– one falsely lulled.  
  
The madness of my people blazed with wisdom and an audaciousness that hated the feeling of hatred itself. Where is that same madness– the madness that gave rise to a King who served as a Shield. The insanity that led to an X becoming a Sword to compliment this Shield.  
  
For fucks’ sake, this madness is one that transcends time and such mortal constructs. A full century before the advent of such figures– Fredrick Douglass and John Brown served as the Shield and Sword of their era, their deaths being aligned with the purpose they served.  
  
For Fredrick’s wick burnt slowly, thus acting as a guide for the people– while John Brown’s wick was akin to a fuse that burnt quickly, the purity behind his conviction led to the ascension of his spirit as his body hung below– becoming a catalyst to the Civil War.  
  
In the advancement of humanity’s self-imposed rights and freedoms, such figures rose up and took on these mantles– while they remain different in many aspects, their overarching goal served as a unification.  
  
For the suffrage of women, there was Cherrie Catt who served as an Umbrella– developing an organization that united women internationally and acted as a cover from the rain of oppression. A woman who maintained her dignity at every turn, one whose words swayed officials– her tone perhaps being more noble than the position that such men held.  
  
Her counterpart was Alice Paul who sacrificed her body and health for this cause– her life only sustained in the fear that her death shall give rise to a martyr, a weeping angel whose tears armed others with a coveted devotion to this cause. For the bruises and brutality she endured  
  
Against senseless bloodshed, the Chicago Eight shed their own blood at the forefront of protests– for how else could its value be appraised if there is none to see? The death of the King only served to sharpen their edge– galvanizing their resolve against a system rigged against them, a violent system that slayed peace yet remains aggrieved at the violence it nurtured– a violence made by the people for the people, a violence that shall never fully dissipate.  
  
In their trials, both retainer and accused fought and were jailed together against the oppression of a judge entrenched in bigotry. A judge who failed their obligations and suppressed the rights that founding fathers died for– a judge hated by an entire city, a hatred upheld by the 11th circuit when they restored these rights and freed both man and statue from the tainted sentences and interpretations.  
  
When I look at today, this madness is missing– entirely absent for I see that wisdom has been replaced with intelligence, that there is a dissonance between the weight of reality and the bubble that those of my generation live in– a bubble that may pop at a moment’s drop, yet the weight of reality is a constant that they live in forgettance of. 

  

The comfort of this bubble only serves to erase the burden that reared the madness of our predecessors– a madness once seen in youthful age shall now make its appearance in our twilight years if we live long enough.  
  
O my people, where has your madness gone? Where has the passion that once moved men beyond their deaths gone? Where have the ideals of our predecessors gone? Am I to believe that more passion could be mustered for mundane discourse than the weights that are forced onto our backs?  
  
I am not afraid to become mad– I am not afraid to be prosecuted or to even bear the pain of death, but I am afraid that my people shall never be blessed with the same madness that our past held.  
  
For I identify myself as a sword rather than a shield– I know that for me to engage with my madness is an act that shall mutilate my lifespan, there is no hearth for me to keep this spark of madness to pass onto another. For the respective swords of my past, there was no need for them to preserve themselves– allowing them to let the madness burn them completely.  
  

Yet I am compelled to sheathe my sword, to dull my words, to become a Shield that I only taint the legacy of. My words cut deep but I am forced to soften them– my heart holds compassion yet my conviction outweighs, a conviction that I am worried will cut allies and foes alike. I am not fit to protect– for I am condemned by sin, consumed by a purity that allows for no compromise– mercy being a virtue that weakens my conviction. It is a virtue that I harbor temporarily– displacing my flame, but once a torchbearer appears, I shall entrust this virtue onto them.   
  
I do not fear my own death, it is an accord that I am ready to face– I am afraid of the flame that shall perish with my passage. I wish for this flame to spread among my people yet I see that we cannot police ourselves, the thought elucidating our senses.  
  
O my people, awaken your senses once more lest you lose the ability to feel entirely. O my people, cast your thoughts outward, to not lose sight of the efforts long fought– to ensure that they do not fall in our cradle.  
  
O my people, I plead that you inherit the flames of those who spent their lives embroiled in conflict– to continue these battles lest we face a repression.  
  
O my people, I plead that you do not lose sight of your empathy– for your laughter is a right that they fought for– a laughter now directed at the depictions of their plights.  
  
O my people, harden your convictions, do not allow yourselves to be swayed by deceivers.  
  
O my people, do not delude yourselves into becoming bystanders when misfortune befalls another. The only difference between you and them is nothing but circumstance.  
  
O My people, I ask that you inherit even a silver of the flame imbued within my writing– to act as a hearth, letting my flame live long after my supposed death. Let this passage comfort my tomb, for these vestiges to keep me warm– a warmth that life failed to deliver me.  
  
I have realised that apart from myself– I can only understand the dead, for the living can change but the dead have already undergone the final change. I can expect no response from the dead yet the lack of response from the living only serves as a laceration on my soul– a pain partially self-inflicted, for I expect nothing from people yet everything from humanity. I find myself disillusioned in the individual yet enamored by the collective– a paradoxical pain that I am to bear unwillingly.  
  

This is the madness that both enfeebles me yet anchors me to humanity, for I love both individual and collective– yet that love is returned by the collective, for their thoughts lie interred and love remains eternal as a remembrance.  
  
O Torchbearer, your existence is an unpromised one, an existence that I remain unaware of– for I may pass long before I unsheathe my Sword, unable to bear witness to your torch. Of us both, you surely are the better one– for your path allows redemption while mine asks for conviction that must not yield. 

  

You are a reformer and I am a man who envisions himself as an avenging angel yet remains as an avenging sinner– my sole purpose is to fight until my body no longer moves, until I am recalled back into the earth. Let your flame become the sky while I am to become a setting sun– to let man adorn themselves as stars in the absence of my light.  
  

For your flame that embodies the gentleness of light, a gentleness of permanence– my flame is the embodiment of the scathing harshness of light, a flame whose end is predetermined. You are a being that could live for the people and I am a being that could die for them– after all, you are sustained by them and I am sustained by my purpose.  
  
I speak not to the individual but the collective, for even my dialect has changed in this regard. The only individual that I know is myself– an eternal truth, for my madness is no different than an insanity that must be dealt with an orderly. My words in a sense are my companionship, for I am my words and my words are but a part of me. I spectate the individual and converse with the collective– an inverted way of perceiving life, a lens that strikes hunger into my vision.  
  
My people, I know that I may not be viewed the same in your eyes– a truth that only you are privy to. That does not stop me from viewing you as my people, for I have few people in the first place. It is futile for me to remember every facet of you, yet I still etch it into my being with my flame.  
  
I may not know your pain, but I shall bear witness to it– to become a testament to it, for pain is a burden meant to be shared. Let it be a burden that I willingly accept than for it to be imposed upon others. For these facets are telling signs– signs that shall guide the edge of my blade.  
  
O my people, truth be told– I do not expect anything from you. I know that my words are no different from ramblings in your mind. I speak to both rationality and madness, in hopes that either one shall heed my words. My expectations lie in your humanity, for the shard within you that echoes with empathy.  
  
I light a torch, a torch that bears a flame distinct from my own– to alight the tinder of those who wish to open their eyes once more. For the height of my flame is far more susceptible to the winds of change than the flame I leave behind.  
  
Let my mercy lay interred in this torch, for a torchbearer to embrace it in its wholeness. Let hatred flow unhindered through my veins, a hatred that seeks to cannibalize its own. A hatred inherited from the dead men whose remains are separated from their motherland– for soldiers that marched in arms against an institution of hate. 

  

A hatred that flew from the quills of writers– words of liberation from oppression, an oppression that seized their lives in the form of a bullet. A hatred born from the suffering of man, a suffering that was deemed as "trivial" by others. A hatred for the apathetic nature of man– a nature that condemned man to complicity.  
  
Let there be no mistake, my hatred is one directed against the very form of hatred– for there is nothing more repugnant than the filth perpetrated by those who harbor discrimination and hatred for their compatriots.  
  
Those who are in dissent of my hatred shall look no further than the mountain of corpses sown in the last century that fought against this hatred. A mountain that contains a myriad of professions, a mountain that contains a love that remains divorced from life.  
  
A single nameless corpse holds more merit than any of you. It seems that some of you are remiss, forgetting that you stand on not mere land– but on the merits of two centuries of men. There are those that concur with my words, yet disagree with my flavour of madness– men who wish to blaze in their own madness, a madness that I may disagree with but shall merge my flame with nevertheless. While we may not claim another, our purpose only serves as our convergence.  
  
For those of you who remain in total dissent against my will against hatred, my words of kindness have long been deposited with the torchbearer. To remain in dissent, I must assume you to be a person that my blade is attracted to. There is no other justification that I could conjure that would eliminate my hostility towards you. Ignorance is a shield that you could no longer claim– I have made it painfully clear of my intentions and the precedent that I seek to uphold.  
  
Let the sights that I have seen, the moments I bore witness to, and the stories that I serve as a record grant me the strength to cut down the hate that you manifest. I am to refute you in every sense of the word, to challenge your thoughts until you can no longer distinguish your moral nature, to burn the forest that you have nurtured– to let new seeds grow anew from the ashes of your hatred.  If I survive this endeavor, then there shall be talk of a future where I sheathe my sword.  
  
I want neither death nor life– but to burn away the hate of a collective, to fertilize the path that my predecessors paved and to clear away that which obstructs my people. Apart from this, I simply wish to remember people, to learn about them– for their simple acts are colourful in itself, especially for a man whose life remains monochrome. For remembrance is an act of love that I shall never relinquish, an act that endears them– a kindle to my flame.  
  
O my people, I invoke your names for the final time. I ask that you do not deny me this act of love. I ask that you shall bury your hatred– for I have no mercy for myself, let alone for others. Hatred and love are two sides of the same coin, a coin that remains upright in my grasp.

  

Let madness make a descent on my people once more– for me to assume stewardship of these flames if my flame no longer has cause to burn. If my madness becomes a sin, let my flames consume me whole– for there is no need for a flame that rebels against its people.  
  
If I remain unextinguished, let me embrace that which I have cast off from myself– to allow myself to become whole again. For a sword’s nature is fragmented, to remain unwhole. Let my flames compensate for a lifetime of apathy, to grant relief to conscience– a span of years becoming tribute.  
  
I may be a fool, for my flame may not exist in this plane– a flame of idealism. I may be a fool, for blinding myself with ideals that shall remain ideals. I am an angry fool– a fool angry at a people that do not recognize me nor acknowledge me. I may be an arrogant fool– a fool who believes every word he writes.  
  
So what? 

  

I am human. A human who wishes to manifest this flame of idealism– to channel it as a sword. Man proposes yet I shall do more than that– for it is my right to manifest this ideal into reality. To feel anger is better than to remain cold– devoid of righteousness. My words are my subjective truth– if I believe in it, that alone is enough.  
  
It is my right, the right of humanity to strive towards their ideals– to defy the terms of reality. My flame is one of defiance. I shall light this flame within my lifetime or surrender myself to death– there is no other option for me. I may compromise my reality, but even in death– my ideals shall not be stained.  
  
I am not the first nor the last to live and die for my ideals. My ideals are but a continuation of the righteous men that came before me. They were trailblazers who created a path for men like me to continue onwards.  
  
One day, the end of this path shall be achieved– a path whose steps are but bodies of these men. Will I reach that end or shall I too become another step on this path?  
  
I have no qualms about either ending– for my path is humanity’s path. I shall either become a vessel of this flame or serve as another piece of tinder for it– I will burn regardless of my fate, the only question is when. 

  

My purpose has been set and my convictions have been written. Dead or alive, I shall scorch humanity’s hatred with my own.

Terminology:  
  
Racism can be divided into two meanings:  
1. (Individual) Discriminatory behaviour by an individual directed at someone else on the basis of their race or ethnicity.  
This can be explicit– showcasing behaviour that clearly deviates from their normality and/or targeted statements. Or it can be implicit– automatic behaviour which is generally harder to detect, impairing a person’s rational judgement without them being aware of it.  
2. (Institution) Policies, laws, or social practices that favours or disfavours a particular group on the basis of race or ethnicity. The explicit and implicit logic used for the individual meaning applies to this.  
  
For the context of my writing, I refer to racism in its individual aspect with both natures. As racism is a subset of discrimination, the broader meaning of discrimination can be inferred from this.  
  
Afterword:  
For starters, the terms “we” and “you” are interchangeable in the context of this piece– meaning that it is directed to myself and the reader. In order to prevent my work from being tainted by interpretations that do not represent my own, I’ll just write it explicitly here.   
  
My intent in writing this is to address the behaviour of certain individuals that engage in discrimination and hide behind the foil of race when it is time for them to be held accountable for their actions. If it wasn’t already obvious, the metric of this discrimination that I refer to in my writing is ethnicity– also known as racism.  
  
I am aware of how my work appears to be targeted. I would like to reaffirm that it is not an attack on identity but one that serves to protect it from the faith of bad actors. While there are many who write about the stereotypical image of racism, I believe that there is not written about its alternative facets, which is why my work is targeted towards this particular facet. While racism is rooted in the concept of ethnicity, it is a concept that can be universally practiced.  
  
While I attribute this attitude to human traits like arrogance and pride in my writing. I believe that there is a sentiment about the term racism that stands to mischaracterize it. That the term racism is not applicable if a member of a minority group is the perpetrator— essentially creating a double standard. This is an ingroup-outgroup bias with the basis being ethnicity, be it conscious or unconscious.  
  
“Ingroup-outgroup” in more simpler terms is when we categorize ourselves into groups with the basis of similarity and then proceed to develop an attitude that favors the group that we identify ourselves to belong to and pits these social groups against each other.  
  
The existence and closeness of such groups is relative to the arbitrariness of the trait used for this basis of similarity. While the weight of these traits are all subjective, some are more subjective than others. Traits such as nationality, ethnicity, religion, beliefs, along with others hold more weight by default– forming a cohesion that can endure the passage of time.  
Traits deemed as frivolous such as tongue length, scalp size, and other such traits hold almost no weight in a normal context– a cohesion that shall never form in the first place, let alone prosper.  
  
To define in-group bias with a minimal word count, the sentence “Us versus Them” would suffice.  
  
My goal of writing this is to reduce the amount of polarization that can commonly be seen in today’s society. A person should be judged individually, rather than collectively. Note that this is a case-by-case basis and that my words may not be appropriate.
