Immortal (仙). Man (亻) and Mountain (山). A being that remains above the mortal world, unblemished by this red dust. A being that wears man's shell, their essence is that of a mountain– one of quiet strength amidst solitude. An Immortal depends on none other than himself and the Heavens with which he communes. 

I am Man (人), but not Mountain (山). I cannot escape this red dust when I am part of it. I am human through and through. As such, I am bound to the same desires and shared nature– even as I carve out my own niche. I aspire to be a mountain yet I find my path blocked by a desire I loathe– companionship. 























It seems that I'm predisposed to burning out. It's annoying, but I'm tired. It's a reoccurring moment in my writing, so I'll paint a picture this time around. I'm writing this with my eyes closed, my head reclining back as the sofa absorbs the abuse I've given my body. Ofcourse, I periodically open them to correct my mistakes– but still. Thankfully, I've written to the point where I've memorized my keyboard layout, so I don't have to open my eyes every few seconds and disturb myself. 

I've taken a good look at others, with their words making it into my essays– [[Conflict]] being my most recent example. But for this essay, we're going back to the root– me. You see, I'm responsible for myself and the path that I've taken in life– both choices and outcomes. I cannot doubt too much and lose my footing entirely, nor can I doubt nothing and break my legs. I'd regret nothing, but I'm human– so I accept what I can and discard the rest. I have no desire to become a man sustained by regrets, nor do I intend on becoming a heartless fiend– even if I recognize my own likeness. 

[[36-Move| To live is to move]], yet every step must be a grounded one– one that can bear my crown. If I want to walk the path that I've envisioned, then I must elucidate the questions that my past self have posed. Sure, there's parts of myself that remain unintelligible, but I can't close my eyes and forget about it. My obligation is to answer. An attempt to answer is still an answer in itself. Our paths are a combination of our choices, so countless branches exist with our choices. Laziness invites complicity. By the time I'm done walking, my path shall be a barren one– killed by this silent poison.

The first question that I must ask: do I need people to live?
